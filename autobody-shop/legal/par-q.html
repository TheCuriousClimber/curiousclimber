Pre-Drive Inspection (Physical Activity Readiness — PAR-Q)

Complete before your first session·Takes about a minute
🧰
Before you rev the engine, run the inspection. Answer the seven questions below honestly. This self-screening is adapted from the widely used PAR-Q and is not a medical exam — it just flags whether you should get a professional check first.

Answer YES or NO to each

  1. Has a doctor ever said that you have a heart condition and that you should only do physical activity recommended by a doctor?
  2. Do you feel pain in your chest when you do physical activity?
  3. In the past month, have you had chest pain when you were not doing physical activity?
  4. Do you lose your balance because of dizziness, or do you ever lose consciousness?
  5. Do you have a bone or joint problem (for example, back, knee, hip, shoulder, elbow or finger) that could be made worse by a change in your physical activity?
  6. Is a doctor currently prescribing medication (for example, for blood pressure or a heart condition)?
  7. Do you know of any other reason why you should not do physical activity?

If you answered YES to one or more

Talk with your doctor before you start, or before you become much more active. Tell them which questions you answered YES to. You may still be able to do any activity you want — as long as you start slowly and build up gradually — or you may need to restrict activity to what is safe for you. Get your doctor’s advice on which programs are suitable.

If you answered NO to all

You can be reasonably sure you can start becoming more physically active — begin slowly and progress gradually, which is the safest way. If your health changes so that you would later answer YES to any of the above, tell a fitness or health professional and ask whether you should adjust your plan.

Delay starting if…

you are feeling unwell — for example with a temporary illness such as a cold or fever — wait until you feel better. If you are or may be pregnant, talk with your doctor before you start.

Extra care for climbing-specific training

Hangboarding, campusing, dynos and heavy pulling place high load on fingers, elbows and shoulders. Even if you clear the questions above, build finger and tendon loading up slowly, respect any history of pulley or tendon injury, and stop at sharp or pinching pain rather than training through it.

Important

This is a self-screening tool for information only; it does not record or transmit your answers and is not a substitute for a medical assessment. If in doubt, get professional clearance.
