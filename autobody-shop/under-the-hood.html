Before you turn the key

Program requirements

A few non-negotiables keep the build safe and the gains real. Meet these before loading up any package.

🩺 Cleared to train

Apparently healthy, injury-free (or cleared by a professional), and comfortable with basic resistance-training movement. When in doubt, get screened first.

🧰 Minimum kit

A hangboard or edge, a pull-up bar, and access to some external load (weights, bands, or a pin-loaded machine). Most packages scale to a home garage.

📓 A way to log

You can't tune what you don't measure. Track your benchmark lifts and re-test every 4–6 weeks so you know the package is working.

Shop recommendations

Run it clean

Prioritise, don't pile on

One or two packages per block. Chasing power, strength, endurance and prehab all at once splits your recovery and stalls every quality. Pick your biggest weak link and drive it hard.

Warm up before max effort

Power and strength work is near-limit. Ramp your fingers, shoulders and system with progressive sets before the heavy stuff — cold engines throw rods.

Rest is part of the tune

Power and strength adaptations need full recovery between efforts and 48 h between hard sessions. Skimping on rest turns a tune-up into overtraining.

Keep the exterior on

Always keep some prehab in the mix. Antagonist and tissue work is cheap insurance — a fast car is worthless while it's up on the lift.

1RM = one-rep maximum. Percentages are of your tested max for that lift. Speed/explosive work is quality-limited — stop the set when output drops, not when you're exhausted.

Across a block

How a build progresses

A tune-up isn't one session — it's a phased block. A typical self-directed cycle runs 4–8 weeks before you re-benchmark and re-choose.

Base

Weeks 1–2. Re-groove technique, build tissue tolerance, and establish working loads at moderate volume.

Load

Weeks 3–5. Push the primary quality — heavier, more explosive, or more sustained depending on the package.

Peak

Weeks 6–7. Lower volume, highest intensity. Sharpen the quality and let fitness surface.

Deload & re-test

Week 8. Back off, recover, then re-benchmark. The numbers decide your next package.

Service desk

Common questions

How many packages should I run at once?

One or two. Your recovery is a finite tank — splitting it across every quality means none of them get enough to adapt. Drive your biggest weak link hard, keep a little prehab in the mix, and rotate.

How do I know which package I need?

Look at how you fall. Blowing a single explosive move? That's a power/horsepower gap. Can't hold the lock-off or tension? Engine. Pumping out on long routes? Fuel tank. Fading over repeated cruxes? Power-endurance. Unsure? Go automatic and let a coach read your tape.

Do I need a gym, or can I do this at home?

Most packages scale to a home setup with a hangboard, a bar and some load (weights or bands). Heavy strength work benefits from more load options, but you can start anywhere.

How often should I re-benchmark?

Every 4–6 weeks, at the end of a block after a short deload. Re-test the benchmarks for the quality you trained — max dyno, 1RMs, hang times, repeats — and let the numbers pick your next tune-up.

Is this climbing-specific or general fitness?

The benchmarks and payoffs here are built around climbing performance — dynos, lock-offs, hangs, cruxes. The training principles (progressive overload, periodization, recovery) are universal, but the targets point at the wall.
