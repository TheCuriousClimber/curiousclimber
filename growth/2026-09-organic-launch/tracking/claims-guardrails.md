# Claims Guardrails (First-Law compliance)

Applies to every article, community post, product page line, and email. A Sonnet agent runs this as a checklist before marking any writing task done.

## Never write
- Numeric outcome promises: "add 20% to your max hang", "two grades in 8 weeks", "guaranteed".
- Medical verbs applied to the product: cure, fix, heal, rehab (the guide is a training program, not a treatment).
- Instructions to train through pain, "push through", or to load a finger that has popped, bowstrung, or swollen.
- Hangboard prescriptions for readers who state they are under ~16 or have been climbing under ~1 year, without first saying the program is not designed for them.
- Absolute physiology ("tendons take exactly X weeks"): use ranges and cite.
- Comparisons that name and disparage other coaches or products.

## Always include
- A clinician route for any pain description: "Pain that persists >48 h, any pop, swelling, or bowstringing → see a physiotherapist/sports physician before loading."
- The pain-monitoring rule used in the guide: loading is acceptable at ≤3/10 pain that settles within 24 h and does not trend upward week to week.
- Progression ceilings: add ≤2–5% of total load (or the smallest increment available) per step; one variable per week.
- Authorship line with credentials on articles: "Written by [Name], MSc Kinesiology, CSEP-CPT. Educational content, not medical advice."
- A citation for every mechanistic claim. If a reference cannot be verified by DOI at writing time, soften the sentence to "evidence suggests" and flag `[VERIFY]` for the human.

## Approved phrasings
- "designed to", "structured to progress", "commonly reported", "in trained climbers, studies have observed…"
- "This block assumes pain-free crimping and at least ~2 years of consistent climbing."
- "I wrote a periodized guide on this (disclosure: it's a paid PDF). Not linking here per sub rules; happy to answer questions in the thread."
