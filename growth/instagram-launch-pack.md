## Carousel 1 — The Tissue Adaptation Mismatch
*Source: articles/finger-anatomy.html + max-hangs-vs-repeaters.html*

1. **Hook:** "Why strong climbers pop pulleys." / Your muscles get ready faster than your tendons.
2. Muscle + nervous system adapt in **weeks**. You *feel* strong fast.
3. Tendons & pulleys (A2/A4) adapt on a **slower timeline** — months, not weeks.
4. That gap = the danger window. You can pull harder than your tissue can safely transmit.
5. The fix isn't "send it." It's loading on the **tissue's timeline**: small steps, one change at a time.
6. Rule of thumb: discomfort ≤3/10 that settles in 24 h and doesn't trend up week to week.
7. Any pop, swelling, or pain past 48 h → see a physio/sports physician before loading again.
8. **CTA:** Not sure you're ready to load? → **Link in bio for the free PDF.**

## Carousel 2 — Max Hangs vs. Repeaters
*Source: articles/max-hangs-vs-repeaters.html*

1. **Hook:** "Max hangs or repeaters?" / Wrong question. They train different things.
2. **Max hangs** = low reps, high force, long rest → mostly a **neural** adaptation (recruitment, rate of force).
3. **Repeaters** = higher volume, shorter rest → mostly **tissue capacity** and work tolerance.
4. Neither is "best." The right pick depends on what you're short on *right now*.
5. New to structured finger work, or building a base? Capacity first (repeaters/density).
6. Solid base, pain-free, chasing a force ceiling? Max hangs — progressed carefully.
7. Best climbers periodize **both** across the year — capacity, then force.
8. **CTA:** Which one is right for you this block? Screen first → **Link in bio for the free PDF.**

## Carousel 3 — 20 mm Edge Testing Rules
*Source: articles/hangboard-weight-progression.html (FAQ) + finger-anatomy.html*

1. **Hook:** "Why everyone tests on a 20 mm edge." / And when you shouldn't chase a number.
2. 20 mm is the research **reference edge**: hard enough to load fingers, not a full crimp. Comparable between people.
3. It's a sensible default — not a rule. Smaller edges are a valid way to add intensity without adding weight.
4. Test in a **half-crimp or open hand**, warm, fresh — never cold, never fatigued.
5. A true max test is itself a near-maximal load. Test **rarely** — end of a block, ~every 6–8 weeks.
6. History of finger pain? Stay at 20 mm+ and progress through **load**, not shrinking the edge.
7. Between tests you don't need a fresh max to progress — reps-in-reserve tells you enough.
8. **CTA:** Ready to test and load? Check the 4 gates first → **Link in bio for the free PDF.**

## Carousel 4 — How Much Weight to Add (and When)
*Source: articles/hangboard-weight-progression.html*

1. **Hook:** "It felt easy, so I added 5 lb." / That instinct is how fingers get hurt.
2. The problem isn't adding weight — it's *how much*, and whether you earned it.
3. **The rule:** add ≤ **2–5%** of total system weight per step (or the smallest increment you have).
4. Only after a session you finished with **reps in reserve** — not after a grind.
5. **One variable per week:** load *or* volume *or* duration. Never all three.
6. A plateau week is cheap. A re-tear is not. When in doubt, hold the number.
7. Pain rule again: ≤3/10, settles in 24 h, no upward trend. Otherwise stop.
8. **CTA:** Progress only makes sense once you're ready to progress → **Link in bio for the free PDF.**

## Carousel 5 — The 4-Gate Readiness Self-Screen (lead magnet)
*Source: downloads/finger-loading-readiness-screen.pdf*

1. **Hook:** "Should you be hangboarding at all?" / Run these 4 gates first. Free.
2. **Gate 1 — Joint health:** any swelling, warmth, or tenderness around a finger joint? That's a flag.
3. **Gate 2 — Climbing age:** under ~1 year consistent, or under 16? Build with climbing first.
4. **Gate 3 — Tolerance:** can you hold a calm 10 s at bodyweight on a 20 mm edge, ≤3/10?
5. **Gate 4 — Workload:** is this week a big spike over your recent normal? Spikes raise risk.
6. Your most cautious answer routes you: **Red** → see a clinician. **Amber** → build capacity. **Green** → load carefully.
