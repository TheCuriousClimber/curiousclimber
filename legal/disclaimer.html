This content is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or personal training. Using it does not create a doctor–patient or coach–client relationship.

✓ What this is

General educational fitness and strength-and-conditioning information, including pre-built programs and exercise demonstrations, for healthy adults who choose to train at their own risk.

Consult a professional first

Always consult a physician or qualified healthcare provider before beginning any exercise program, especially if you are pregnant or postpartum, are over 40 and inactive, have (or a family history of) heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, joint or bone problems, a chronic condition, or any symptoms of concern. Complete our Physical Activity Readiness screening (PAR-Q) before you start — if it flags anything, get medical clearance first.

You assume the risks of exercise

Physical activity carries inherent risks, including muscle strains, sprains, falls, cardiovascular events, and in rare cases serious injury or death. By using any program or content from The Kinesiology of Climbing, you acknowledge these risks and agree that you participate voluntarily and at your own risk, to the fullest extent permitted by law (see the Terms of Service).

Stop and seek help if…

Stop exercising immediately and seek medical attention if you experience chest pain or pressure, pain radiating into the arm or jaw, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, faintness, an irregular or racing heartbeat, sudden severe joint or muscle pain, or any symptom that alarms you. In an emergency, call your local emergency number ([e.g. 911 / 112 / 999]) right away. Train within your ability, warm up, use appropriate loads, and never push through sharp pain.

Scale to your own level

Every exercise in our programs includes easier (regression) and harder (progression) options. Choose the version and load that let you keep good form, and progress gradually. Just because a program lists an exercise does not mean it is right for you on a given day.

Individual results vary

Fitness outcomes depend on many factors (starting point, effort, consistency, genetics, sleep, nutrition, and more). We make no guarantee of any specific result, and any examples are illustrative, not promises.

Demonstration links point to third-party videos (e.g. on YouTube) that we do not own or control. They are provided for convenience only; we are not responsible for their content, accuracy, or availability, and their inclusion is not an endorsement.
